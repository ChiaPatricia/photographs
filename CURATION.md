# Curation

How the photographs were chosen, ordered and named, and where every fact comes from.
Everything here is a draft until Jia says otherwise.

## The archive

75 photographs from one day, **18 October 2026**, in the Hudson Valley: the Lyndhurst mansion in
Tarrytown around noon, maple foliage, Sleepy Hollow village in the afternoon, the river at Kingsland
Point until after sunset, and the Great Jack O'Lantern Blaze at night.

- Canon EOS 70D with EF-S 18–200mm, files in Adobe RGB. The camera clock runs **12 hours ahead**
  (its sunset is at "06:03"; the street clock in `_MG_2884` reads 4:10 when the file says "04:10"),
  so all times are corrected by −12 h in `content/site.yaml`.
- Olympus μ1020 (night photographs, 1280 px). Now all held back.

Added later: seven iPhone 13 Pro photographs of the **aurora over southern Maine**, 10 October 2024,
22:01–22:14 EDT (Display P3; the phone's clock is right, and its GPS is never published).

What runs through almost every frame: **light from behind** — leaves lit through, sun on the
water, people in silhouette — and orange against blue.

## Five collections

The logic of the names: one plain word in each language, not translations of each other, and one
sentence that tells something the photographs cannot show.

### 01 · Turning · 转色 — leaves (9)

Sequenced from green to red, ending on a single leaf held up in a hand.

> The yellow is in the leaf all summer, hidden by the green; the red is made new in autumn, and botanists still disagree about why.
>
> 黄色整个夏天都在叶子里，只是被绿色盖住了；红色要到秋天才新合成，为什么，科学家至今还在争论。

- Carotenoids are present all season and unmasked as chlorophyll breaks down; anthocyanins are
  synthesised in autumn. Archetti et al. 2009, *Trends Ecol Evol* 24:166, doi:10.1016/j.tree.2008.10.006;
  Schaberg et al. 2008, *Trees* 22:573 (USDA Forest Service).
- Why trees make red is still debated (photoprotection vs signalling): Hamilton & Brown 2001,
  *Proc R Soc B* 268:1489; Renner & Zohner 2019, *New Phytol* 224:1464; Pena-Novas & Archetti 2020,
  *J Evol Biol* 33:1669; Hughes et al. 2021, *New Phytol* 233:22.

### 02 · Cache · 藏 — squirrel, acorns, a bird hidden in leaves, the mockingbird (4)

> With other squirrels nearby, a grey squirrel will sometimes dig a hole, cover it up, and leave nothing inside.
>
> 附近有别的松鼠时，灰松鼠有时会挖一个坑，再填好，里面什么也没放。

- "Deceptive caching": Steele et al. 2008, *Animal Behaviour* 75:705, doi:10.1016/j.anbehav.2007.07.026 —
  about 13% of caching events, more often with other squirrels within ~20 m. The audience is other
  squirrels, not people; do not say they "fool watching humans".
- Note on `_MG_3068`: a male northern mockingbird may learn around 200 songs (Cornell Lab, All About Birds).
- Note on `_MG_3056`: "There is a bird in this one." (It is small, among the leaves.)

### 03 · Glitter · 波光 — the river, from bright afternoon to after sunset (10)

> In 1951 two oceanographers photographed the sun's glitter from a B-17 off Maui, and found that it spreads wider as the wind rises.
>
> 1951年，两位海洋学家从毛伊岛外的一架B-17上拍摄海面的阳光反光，发现风越大，这片光铺得越开。

- Cox & Munk 1954, "Measurement of the roughness of the sea surface from photographs of the sun's
  glitter", *J Opt Soc Am* 44:838, doi:10.1364/JOSA.44.000838. Flights from a B-17G off Maui,
  25 Aug – 25 Sep 1951; mean-square slope grows linearly with wind speed. Munk 2009,
  *Annu Rev Mar Sci* 1:377.

### 04 · Still · 还在 — lighthouses, Gothic windows, a dining room, a lamp, the moon, the bridge at dusk (8)

"Still": still here, stillness, a still photograph. 还在: still here.

> This lighthouse was retired in the 1960s because of a new bridge downriver. That bridge is gone now; the lighthouse is still here.
>
> 这座灯塔六十年代就退役了，因为下游修了座新桥。后来那座桥拆了，灯塔还在。

(Rewritten in plain words after Jia found the first, compressed version confusing.)

- Tarrytown (Sleepy Hollow) Light, first lit 1 October 1883; the Tappan Zee Bridge (opened 1955)
  made it obsolete (US Coast Guard history: history.uscg.mil, "Tarrytown Lighthouse"). The 1955
  bridge was dismantled 2017–19 after the Governor Mario M. Cuomo Bridge — the one in these
  photographs — opened beside it. The lighthouse was restored in 2024.
- Avoid: an exact retirement year (sources say 1961 or 1965) and "68 years of service" (wrong).

### 05 · Slow · 慢 — the aurora over southern Maine (4)

Opens on the red, so the sentence about the red follows it; ends looking straight up.

> This red comes from oxygen that takes about two minutes to glow; only very high up, where the air is thin, can it wait that long.
>
> 这种红光来自氧原子，它要等大约两分钟才发光；只有在很高、空气很稀薄的地方，它才等得起。

- The red is the 630 nm line of atomic oxygen, O(¹D). Its radiative lifetime is about 110 s; lower
  down, collisions take the energy away before it can be emitted, so red aurora comes from roughly
  150–250 km and up, and the green (lifetime about a second) from 100–150 km. Atmospheric Optics,
  "Glowing Gases – Aurorae" (atoptics.co.uk/highsky/auror3.htm); Solomon, Hays & Abreu 1988,
  "The auroral 6300 Å emission: Observations and modeling", *J Geophys Res* 93(A9):9867,
  doi:10.1029/JA093iA09p09867; DLR, "Green auroras" (100–120 km).
- The storm: an X1.8 flare peaked at 9:56 p.m. EDT on 8 October 2024 (NASA SVS 14703); its CME
  arrived at 11:15 a.m. EDT on 10 October and the storm reached G4, "severe", at 12:57 p.m.
  (NOAA SWPC, reported by *Astronomy*). These photographs are from 22:01–22:14 on 10 October —
  two days after the flare, at almost the same time of night.
- Avoid: an exact altitude for where the red starts (sources say 150–200 km), and saying the light
  "came from the sun" — the sun's eruption set it off; the light itself is our own air.

## Verified stories not used (for later collections)

Each was checked against primary or authoritative sources; corrections noted.

- **七十二候.** 18 Oct 2026 is the first day of 寒露·末候「菊有黄华」(寒露 began 8 Oct, 14:29 Beijing time).
  「腐草为萤」is 大暑·初候 (《逸周书·时训解》「大暑之日，腐草化为萤」): right about when, wrong about why —
  firefly eggs and larvae live in damp leaf litter (Riley et al. 2021, *PeerJ* 9:e12121).
  「雀入大水为蛤」is 寒露·次候.
- **Henry Hudson, 1609.** Sailed up this river hoping it was a way to Asia; reached the Tappan Zee on
  14 September. (Used briefly for the fourth collection, dropped with the name 水路.)
- **North Tarrytown → Sleepy Hollow.** Voted 10 Dec 1996, 1,304 to 710, months after the GM plant closed.
  "Settled in 1640" on the sign has no documented source. (Dropped with the Halloween photographs.)
- **Crows and the caveman mask.** Seven crows trapped in 2006 at the University of Washington by people
  in a caveman mask; crows that were never trapped learned to scold it too (Cornell, Marzluff &
  Pecoraro 2012, *Proc R Soc B* 279:499).
- **Christmas Bird Count.** Frank Chapman proposed counting birds on Christmas Day 1900 instead of the
  "side hunt" (*Bird-Lore* 2:192). In Chinese photography slang 打鸟 means photographing birds — some
  bird-watchers dislike the word.
- **Sunflecks.** Seconds-long flecks of sun can supply 10–80% of a forest-floor plant's daily light
  (Chazdon 1986, *J Ecol*; Pfitsch & Pearcy 1989, *Oecologia*).
- **Crown shyness.** Tethering lodgepole pines so they could not collide raised crown cover 14.4% in six
  years vs 2.1% in controls (Meng et al. 2006, *J Ecol* 94:681). Putz et al. 1984 was observational.
- **Purkinje, 1825.** Before dawn, reds look darkest and blue is noticed first. No flowers or geraniums
  in his text; "1819" is unsupported.
- **Linnaeus' flower clock** (*Philosophia Botanica*, 1751): 46 species listed, 43 arranged by hour;
  never planted by him; depends on latitude.
- Also checked: Mozart's starling (27 May 1784, 34 kreuzer, "Das war schön!"), Bessel's parallax of
  61 Cygni (1838), Akasegawa's Thomasson (named 1982), Shklovsky's misspelled *ostranenie*
  (1983, "like a dog with a cut-off ear"), 竺可桢's diary (1 Jan 1936 – 6 Feb 1974, last line begins 「局报」),
  佛光寺 (1937; 林徽因 first read 宁公遇's name), 二十四番花信风 (王逵《蠡海集》).
- Not usable: Agloe's "paper town" origin (folklore), Pando's age (disputed), the Uexküll tick's
  18 years (his own report only).
